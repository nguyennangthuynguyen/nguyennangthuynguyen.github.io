Research

Summary of research interests

My research looks at how sustainability and digital technology change the way firms and consumers make decisions in emerging markets, especially Vietnam. On the consumer side, I study how brand transparency and corporate digital responsibility shape motivation, trust and sustainable purchase intention. On the firm side, I study how green banking policy and AI adoption affect bank performance, and how Vietnamese SMEs can turn AI and sustainability knowledge into competitive advantage. I mainly use quantitative methods and combine them with interviews when building a model.

    Dinh, V. H., Vu, T. T. L., Le, A. Q., & Nguyen, N. T. N. (2025)

    “Tính minh bạch thương hiệu và hành vi mua sản phẩm bền vững: Góc nhìn từ động lực xanh và giá trị cảm xúc” [Brand transparency and sustainable purchasing behavior: Perspectives from green motivation and emotional value]

    • Sustainability
    • Consumer behavior
    Published · Journal of Asian Business and Economic Studies (JABES), 36(1), 90–106

    n = 357 · 3 regions of Vietnam · PLS-SEM · Second Prize, national award 2025

    DOI: 10.24311/jabes/2025.36.1.08

    See moreSee less

    Using 357 consumer responses from across Vietnam and PLS-SEM, the study shows that brand transparency raises consumers’ intention to buy sustainable products, their green extrinsic motivation and the emotional value they feel. Green extrinsic motivation carries part of the effect of transparency on purchase intention, while emotional value does not act as a significant mediator. The wider award-winning project behind the paper also used ANN, PROCESS Macro and fsQCA.

    Second Prize, Ministerial-Level Science and Technology Award for University Students 2025 · First Prize, FTU Student Research Contest 2025 · Semi-final, Eureka Scientific Research Award 2025

  3. [3]

    Nguyen, T. Q., Le, T. H. T., Nguyen, N. T. N., Doan, V. T. V., & Mai, T. L. T.

    “The Impact of Green Credit Policy and Artificial Intelligence Transformation on the Operations of Vietnamese Commercial Banks”

    • Green finance
    • AI
    • Banking
    Presented · ICFAA 2026, National Economics University, Hanoi, 15 Oct 2026

    19 banks · 2015–2024 panel · random-effects regression

    See moreSee less

    With an unbalanced panel of 19 Vietnamese commercial banks (2015–2024) and a random-effects model with year fixed effects, the study tests how AI, fintech and green banking policies relate to bank profitability. AI is linked to higher ROE, while the first green banking decision (1408/QĐ-NHNN) is linked to lower returns and the later, broader decision (1604/QĐ-NHNN) to higher ROE and ROA, which suggests that implementation matters more than formal adoption. I collected the panel data, ran the regressions, wrote the results section and presented the paper.

    • Digital responsibility

    n = 600 · 2×2 experiment · Gen Z and Gen X

    A 2×2 between-subjects experiment on how firms' practices in digital environments shape consumer responses. I collected and cleaned 600 responses from Gen Z and Gen X participants and test mediation and cohort–treatment interactions with PROCESS Models 4 and 8 and two-way ANOVA.

EDTECH STARTUP · 2026

Orango

  • EdTech
  • AI
  • Startup

Founder, research and product development

Students now use ChatGPT and other AI tools for almost every IELTS task, while teachers with 20 or more students per class cannot see who actually understands the lesson. Orango is an AI teaching assistant built into a learning management system for English centers and schools. It gives students hints instead of ready-made answers, follows each center's own curriculum, flags repeated mistakes and how much each student relies on AI, and sends clear progress reports to teachers and parents. I lead a team of four. Orango was named one of the Top 10 Outstanding Projects at the TEC Go Startup Program, Foreign Trade University (2026).

NHA TRANG UNIVERSITY · 2025

Science and Technology Award for University Students

Final round and award ceremony

For more than a year, our team studied how brand transparency shapes sustainable buying. I helped collect survey responses in all three regions of Vietnam and learned to combine SEM, neural networks, PROCESS and fsQCA, using each method to check the others. The final round and award ceremony in Nha Trang crowned that year.

The bigger lesson came afterwards. Feedback from an economist at a Japanese university showed me that surveys like ours cannot separate a capability from the firms that have it, and that is why I now want to learn causal methods.
